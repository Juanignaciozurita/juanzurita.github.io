\begin{frame}{Why Heterogeneous Agents?}
Representative-agent models miss:

\bigskip
\begin{itemize}
\item Wealth inequality and its aggregate effects
\item Differential responses to policy across the distribution
\item Precautionary saving and liquidity constraints
\end{itemize}

\bigskip
But HA models have a \alert{high-dimensional} state: the entire wealth distribution $\Gamma_t$.

Traditional methods: approximate $\Gamma_t$ with a few moments (Krusell \& Smith, 1998).
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Krusell--Smith (1998)}
\begin{block}{Key insight}
Approximate the distribution by its \textbf{mean} (aggregate capital $K$). Agents' decision rules depend on $(a_i, z_i, K, Z)$.
\end{block}

\bigskip
Algorithm:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Guess a law of motion: $\ln K' = b_0 + b_1 \ln K$ (conditional on $Z$)
\item Solve individual problems given this law
\item Simulate the economy and update $b_0, b_1$
\item Iterate until convergence
\end{enumerate}

\bigskip
This works because $R^2 \approx 0.999$ --- the mean is sufficient.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{DEQNs for Heterogeneous Agents}
Replace the parametric law of motion with a \textbf{neural network}:

$$K' = \mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(K, Z)$$

\bigskip
Advantages:
\begin{itemize}
\item Can capture non-linear dynamics traditional regressions miss
\item Extend to many moments without specifying functional forms
\item Scale to models where ``approximate aggregation'' fails
\end{itemize}

\bigskip
Scheidegger and collaborators show DEQNs can handle state spaces with $50+$ dimensions.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Young's Histogram Method}
To simulate the distribution:

\bigskip
\begin{enumerate}
\item Discretise the individual state space into bins
\item Track the mass in each bin over time
\item Update masses using the policy function
\end{enumerate}

\bigskip
This avoids simulating millions of individual agents. Combined with DEQNs, the entire solution is differentiable.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Summary}
\begin{itemize}
\item HA models are essential for inequality and policy analysis
\item The state space is the entire wealth distribution --- very high-dimensional
\item DEQNs overcome the curse of dimensionality
\item Krusell--Smith with neural networks: more flexible, more scalable
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}
